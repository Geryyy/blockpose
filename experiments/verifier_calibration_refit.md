# `dino_verifier` calibration temperature: refit and diagnosis

Phase 2a of the production-readiness audit. Tool: `tools/refit_verifier_calibration.py`.
Reproduce with:

```
python -m tools.refit_verifier_calibration --output out/verifier_calibration_refit.json
```

Data: `out/rgb_candidate_prior_seibersdorf` (524 exported records, 509 with a
`positive`/`negative` label after dropping 15 `ignore`; 273 positive / 236
negative), already produced by `experiments.rgb_candidate_prior.export` --
not regenerated by this work. Checkpoint: `models/production/dino_verifier`
(`calibration_temperature = 12.497270584106445`). Folds: the 24
leave-one-scenario-out development folds in
`config/rgb_ablation_splits_seibersdorf.yaml`, resolved with
`experiments.fold_safety.development_fold_of_snapshot`/`select_development_groups`
(17 of them have at least one labelled sample after export/label filtering).

## Headline result: the refit temperature is *larger*, not smaller

This is the opposite of what the audit's framing (small `T` -> useless
compressed contribution, so refit to something smaller and more useful)
would predict, and the reason is upstream of calibration:

| | temperature | NLL | ECE | Brier | contribution span, raw grid 0.01..0.99 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| shipped (`config.json` today) | 12.497 | 0.7354 | 0.1727 | 0.2707 | 0.0727 |
| global refit, in-sample | 46488 | 0.6931 | 0.0364 | 0.2500 | 0.0000198 |
| LOSO-nested refit, per-fold mean (range) | 42111 (16795-80231) | -- | -- | -- | ~0 |
| LOSO-nested refit, **pooled held-out** | -- | 0.6931 | 0.0364 | 0.2500 | -- |

("LOSO-nested" = for each of the 17 scenario folds with labelled data, fit
`T` on every *other* fold's raw scores/labels with
`experiments.rgb_candidate_prior.model.fit_temperature`, apply it only to
the held-out fold, pool the held-out predictions across folds.)

`0.6931 = ln(2)`, i.e. the NLL of predicting a flat 0.5 for every sample
regardless of label. **Every honestly-refit temperature converges to the
same place as "ignore the head entirely and predict 50/50"**, and gets there
by driving `T` two to three orders of magnitude larger than the shipped
value, not smaller.

### Do not read a magnitude into those refit `T` values

They are not estimates of anything. `tools/refit_verifier_calibration.py`'s
`temperature_objective_scan` sweeps `T` over `1e-3 .. 1e9` and reports
`strictly_decreasing_in_temperature: true`: for this score distribution the
BCE objective `fit_temperature` minimizes **has no finite minimizer**. It
falls monotonically toward its infimum `ln(2)`, reached only as
`T -> infinity`. So `46488`, the per-fold `16795-80231` range, and the
`42111 +/- 16693` mean/std above are all just the point where LBFGS stopped
descending a ramp -- change the iteration budget and they change. The
*finding* is the divergence, not its size; the fold-to-fold spread carries no
information about the data and should not be quoted as if it did.

The sharpest form of the result needs no fitted `T` at all: the scan reports
`nll_of_best_constant_predictor = 0.6905` (the base rate, 53.6% positive)
against `nll_limit_as_temperature_to_infinity = ln(2) = 0.6931`. Even in the
limit, **no temperature whatsoever beats ignoring the head and predicting the
base rate.** That is a statement about the whole one-parameter family, not
about any particular fit of it.

## Why: the raw scores are compressed near 0, not spread across the range

The audit's illustrative figure (T=12.497 maps raw 0.01..0.99 to calibrated
0.409..0.591) assumed raw scores actually range that widely. They don't.
Querying the shipped head's actual raw output on all 509 labelled samples:

```
raw score min/max:  0.0000445 / 0.0195
positive-label mean/std: 0.00214 / 0.00246
negative-label mean/std: 0.00112 / 0.00173
rank AUC (raw score vs. label, pooled across all folds/blocks): 0.731
```

The head's raw `sigmoid(mlp(features))` output never leaves the bottom 2%
of `[0, 1]` for any of the 509 labelled candidates checked. That is expected
given how it is trained: `train.py` trains the MLP with
`ranking_margin_loss` (a pairwise hinge over each candidate set), which only
asks positives to outrank negatives *within a set* by a margin -- it has no
term that pushes absolute scores toward 0 or 1, so nothing stops the whole
population from sitting in a tight, arbitrarily-located sliver near 0. That
sliver still has real signal (AUC 0.731, well above chance), but at a
location temperature scaling cannot exploit:

`sigmoid(logit(raw)/T)` is a pure *rescaling* of `logit(raw)` -- it has no
intercept. `logit(raw)` here sits around -10 to -4 (well negative of 0, the
decision boundary), so no positive finite `T` can recenter it; increasing
`T` only pulls every prediction toward 0.5 from below. With a raw-score
distribution this systematically shifted, temperature scaling degenerates
exactly the way it did here: the honest BCE-optimal `T` under this
one-parameter family is enormous, because "predict 0.5 for everyone" beats
every single-scalar-temperature alternative on log-loss for this
distribution.

**A model with an intercept fixes this in principle.** Fitting a 2-parameter
Platt scaling (`sigmoid(a * logit(raw) + b)`, same 509 samples, in-sample --
`platt_reference_fit`) gives `a = 0.795, b = 5.732`, NLL 0.6087 -- meaningfully
better than both the shipped 0.7354 *and* the ln(2) = 0.6931 floor that pure
temperature scaling is stuck at. The intercept `b = 5.73` is doing the work:
it is what recenters the logit distribution before the head's real
(AUC 0.73) discriminative signal can be exploited. This is diagnostic
evidence, not a proposed change -- `blockpose.rgb_verifier._calibrate_score`
is a single-scalar-temperature contract by design (it must stay
torch-free and importable without the training stack), and changing that
contract is out of scope here.

## Realized contribution span (using the head's actual raw range, not a grid)

The audit's 0.01..0.99 grid overstates the shipped calibration's practical
damage in one respect and understates it in another. Using the head's
*actual* observed raw range (0.0000445 to 0.0195) instead of the illustrative
grid, at the shipped `T = 12.497`:

```
raw 0.0000445  -> calibrated 0.3096 -> contribution -0.0761
raw 0.0195     -> calibrated 0.4223 -> contribution -0.0311
```

Every realized contribution is **negative**, regardless of the sample's true
label -- the verifier can never hand out positive evidence for any candidate
in this export, only a smaller or larger penalty (-0.076 to -0.031, a
realized span of 0.045). This does not break within-set ranking (`_calibrate_score`
is strictly monotonic in `raw`, so relative order among candidates scored
in the same call is preserved -- see "What this script does NOT claim" in
`tools/refit_verifier_calibration.py`), but it means the DINO contribution
acts as a small, always-negative nudge, not the signed evidence the design
doc's `[-0.20, +0.20]` contract implies it should be.

## Was the shipped 12.497 fit on genuinely held-out predictions?

**Yes -- this part of the process was not the bug.** Read
`experiments/rgb_candidate_prior/train.py`:

- `train.py` trains one fresh MLP head per development fold held out (its
  main cross-validation loop, no `--held-out-fold` given), evaluates each
  head only on the fold it did *not* train on, and pools those held-out
  `(score, label)` pairs across every fold (`pooled_scores`/`pooled_labels`,
  lines ~359-375).
- `fit_temperature` (`experiments/rgb_candidate_prior/model.py`) is then
  called once on that pooled, genuinely-held-out set (train.py line 394),
  and the result is what gets saved into the deployed checkpoint via
  `save_checkpoint` (trained on the union of all folds, since no further
  held-out slice remains once cross-validation is done) with
  `"calibration_fit_on": "pooled_cross_validated_held_out_predictions"`.
- Comparing checkpoints directly confirms this is exactly the run that
  produced the shipped artifact: `out/dino_verifier_checkpoint_seibersdorf/mlp_head.pt`
  and `models/production/dino_verifier/mlp_head.pt` are byte-identical
  (md5 `c854a7e7373b9b77b5b19942467c02e5`), and
  `out/dino_verifier_checkpoint_seibersdorf/train_summary.json` records
  `"calibration_temperature": 12.497270584106445` with
  `"calibration_fit_on": "pooled_cross_validated_held_out_predictions"` and
  `"development_folds"` listing 19 Seibersdorf scenario fold ids
  (`[0..11, 15, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 23]`; the 17 this report refits on are
  those minus folds 2 and 3, whose only exported samples are `ignore`),
  i.e. it already used the leave-one-scenario-out structure, not the old
  two-fold `capture_batch_a/b` manifest its own docstring/note text still
  references (that note text is stale, not the actual split used).

So the "red flag" framing in the task ("a temperature that large ... suggests
the head's logits were near-degenerate when the temperature was fit") is
correct about the symptom (near-degenerate logits) but the mechanism is not
a fold-safety bug -- it is what any correctly-fit single-scalar temperature
*must* do to a ranking-trained head whose raw scores never leave a narrow
sliver away from the decision boundary. Refitting more carefully (this
report's LOSO-nested version) does not recover a smaller, more useful `T`;
it diverges instead.

Why does it diverge here when `train.py`'s fit returned a finite 12.497 from
the same procedure? Not because of fold size -- the global refit on all 509
pooled samples diverges identically, so data quantity is not the mechanism.
The two fits ran on *different score populations*: `train.py` calibrated the
pooled held-out predictions of 19 separately retrained per-fold heads,
while this report calibrates the one frozen shipped head (see "In-sample raw
scores, by design" in `tools/refit_verifier_calibration.py`). Those per-fold
heads' pooled scores were not retained, so this report cannot say whether
their objective had a finite minimum or whether 12.497 is itself a stopping
point on the same ramp -- in a synthetic replay of this regime (logits
uniform on -10..-4, base rate 0.536) `fit_temperature` returns 4.7, 26.4 and
456871 at `max_iter` 20, 50 and 200 respectively, so a finite-looking 12.497
is entirely consistent with either. Either way it does not rescue the shipped
value: at `T = 12.497` the shipped head's actual predictions score NLL
0.7354, worse than both `ln(2)` and the 0.6905 base-rate constant. The
calibration was fit honestly, on a distribution the checkpoint it was saved
into does not reproduce.

## Recommendation

1. **Do not ship a larger refit `T`.** It is honestly worse than the status
   quo for this purpose: it drives the contribution span to ~0 (from an
   already-thin 0.073 down to ~0.00002), i.e. it would make the verifier
   contribute *nothing* rather than a small penalty. Do not overwrite
   `models/production/dino_verifier/config.json` with any of this report's
   refit numbers.
2. **The 12.497 value is not the bug to fix; the calibration family is.**
   Single-scalar temperature scaling is structurally the wrong tool for a
   ranking-trained head whose raw scores are not centered near the decision
   boundary. If the DINO verifier's additive contribution is meant to carry
   real signed evidence, `blockpose.rgb_verifier._calibrate_score` would
   need an intercept term (Platt-style `sigmoid(a * logit(raw) + b)`) or the
   MLP head would need a training-time term that anchors its absolute scale
   (e.g. a BCE component alongside the ranking loss), not a different
   scalar `T`. Either is a real code change to `rgb_verifier.py`'s
   calibration contract and to `train.py`'s training loss, and belongs to
   a separate, deliberately-scoped piece of work -- not folded into this
   refit.
3. **Ranking use of the verifier is unaffected either way.** `_calibrate_score`
   is strictly monotonic in `raw_score` for any `T > 0`, so nothing here
   changes whether the DINO verifier can tell candidates apart within one
   scored set (AUC 0.731 on this export) -- only whether its *additive
   scalar contribution* carries usable magnitude. Do not read this report as
   "the verifier is broken."
4. The shipped checkpoint is Git-LFS-tracked; whether/when to touch it is an
   orchestrator decision, not made here.

## Files

- `tools/refit_verifier_calibration.py` -- the refit/diagnostic tool this
  report is generated from. Reuses `experiments/fold_safety.py`'s
  `development_fold_of_snapshot`/`select_development_groups` for fold
  resolution (never string-matches `split_group`) and
  `experiments/rgb_candidate_prior/model.py`'s `fit_temperature`/
  `apply_calibration` so the fitting math matches `train.py` exactly. Reuses
  the export's cached region features (`dino_verifier_features.npz`) rather
  than re-running the frozen DINOv2 backbone; spot-checked bit-identical
  against `DinoVerifierHead.forward` on a sample (see the script's
  docstring). Every number quoted in this report -- including the raw-score
  distribution, the rank AUC, the realized (non-grid) contribution span, the
  objective scan and the Platt reference fit -- is emitted by that one
  command, so this report can be re-derived rather than trusted. Refuses to
  run (exit 1) if
  `out/rgb_candidate_prior_seibersdorf` is missing, rather than silently
  regenerating a fresh (and differently-sized) export.
